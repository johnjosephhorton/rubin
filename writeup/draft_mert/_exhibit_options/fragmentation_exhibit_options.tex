\newcommand{\sharedparams}{Every step has manual cost $(\manualSkill{i},\manualTime{i})=(1,5)$ and AI management cost $(\AIskill{i},\AItime{i})=(1,6)$; easy steps succeed with probability $q_i=1$ and hard steps with $q_i=0.1$. The two arrangements therefore differ only in \emph{where} the AI-able steps sit, not in any cost primitive.}

\newcommand{\sharedreading}{Top layer: each step's difficulty for AI (teal = easy, red = hard). Bottom layer: its execution in the cost-minimizing strategy (gray = manual, green = automated, orange = augmented). In the dispersed arrangement no two easy steps are adjacent, so no chain can form and every step is manual, for a total cost of $5m$. In the clustered arrangement the easy steps form a contiguous run that the firm executes as one AI chain, verified once at its augmented endpoint, for a total cost of $6+5m/2$, strictly below $5m$ whenever $m>2$.}

\newcommand{\exlead}{Consider the same set of steps as in Example~2, but now cluster the
AI-friendly steps: the first $m/2$ steps are easy (each succeeds with probability
$q_i = 1$) and the last $m/2$ are hard (each succeeds with probability $q_i = 0.1$).}

\newcommand{\extail}{Now the easy steps form a contiguous run, so the firm can chain
them: it automates the first $m/2 - 1$ at zero direct cost and verifies only once, at the
end of the run. Because every easy step succeeds on the first attempt, the entire run of
$m/2$ easy steps collapses to the single management cost of its augmented endpoint,
$\AIskill{m/2}\AItime{m/2}/\prod_{i=1}^{m/2} q_i = 6$.}
